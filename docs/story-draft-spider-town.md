# The Longmother — story pitch

## Stance on "Undearth" (struck 2026-10-10)

I am **consciously avoiding** reviving Undearth as a named underworld/missing-sun cosmology. It was cut two days before this brief, and bolting it back on under a new coat of paint would second-guess that call rather than respect it. Instead I keep the one evocative image that made it compelling — a world under permanent shadow — but ground it in something diegetic and small: the shadow is cast by the spider herself, not by a missing sun. No cosmic event, no underworld plane, nothing that needs its own mythology scene we don't have room to tell in crawl text. If the owner wants Undearth back wholesale, that's a one-line override (see Open Questions).

## 1. The core premise

**The Longmother** is a spider the size of a hill range — old enough that nobody alive remembers her hatching, large enough that her back has been built on for generations. The town isn't *near* her; it **is** her topside: streets laid across carapace plates, wagons lashed between her joints, the caravan's shops literally the oldest, broadest plates she's shed and the town has re-decked.

She walks because **standing ground goes bad**. Somewhere under the world a slow blight is souring the earth — crops fail, wells turn, the dead don't stay down — and it only ever catches up with things that hold still. The Longmother has outrun it for longer than anyone's history, which is the only argument anyone needs for why the town keeps moving instead of settling: every town that didn't is a ruin you walk past (Veshker Ruins is one of them). She isn't running *toward* anywhere in particular. She is running so the blight behind her never finishes catching up.

The danger "down there" is what her own footfalls stir up. Each step breaks ground that's been undisturbed — sometimes for centuries — and whatever the blight has been incubating under it comes up through the crack: vermin, rot, things that used to be people. The topside town never sees it. The space between her legs and the raw ground at each new footfall does.

## 2. Why the heroes can survive down there

The party aren't chosen ones; they're the **vanguard crews** whose job is to drop down at each footfall, clear what the step disturbed before it can climb up a support cable, and salvage whatever the broken ground turned up (ore, water, relics) before she walks on. Three concrete reasons they can do this and a townsperson can't:

- **Their gear is grown, not mined.** Armor plates and weapons are forged from the Longmother's own molted carapace — lighter and tougher than anything smelted from sour ground-ore, but it wears and *shatters* under load exactly like chitin does, which is the weapon-durability/shatter-rebound mechanic already in the game. It isn't infinite; it's re-forged from the next molt, which is why the blacksmith wagon matters as much as the merchant.
- **They fight the way her own brood does.** Hatchling spiders immobilize prey by flanking it from two sides against something that can't move — the **pincer** is a technique scouts copied from watching her young hunt, not a swordsman's invention. It only works on things that can be boxed in against an unbroken line, same as in a nest.
- **They move on her clock, not their own.** The footfall gives a window, not a schedule — you get exactly as long as it takes her leg to plant, and the ground finishes stirring up its hazard whether you've acted or not. That's the act-advanced Timeline: the clock doesn't run while you think, but it isn't yours either — it's hers, and every committed action is you keeping pace with a stride that will not slow down for you.

A caravan that stops being able to field vanguard crews is a caravan that has to start skipping digs — which starves the forges, which is losing slowly, which is the actual stakes of failing at Camp more than any single lost battle.

## 3. The five themes, reframed as a route

*(Each paragraph is crawl-length — a stand-in for the Crawl field in campaign.json, not final copy.)*

**Green Valley** — The last green ground on the old road, and it's starting to go: the wells have a taste to them, and the frogs that used to keep to the marsh edge are coming up onto dry land in numbers nobody's seen. This is departure, not arrival — the valley isn't under attack, it's the first proof that *staying* was never actually safe, it just hadn't caught up yet. The crawl can do double duty establishing the caravan's routine (footfall, dig, salvage, move on) for a player who's never seen it.

**Sandsea Reaches** — Sun-bleached country the Longmother has crossed before, maybe more than once — old wagon ruts under the dunes that aren't hers. The sand keeps what it swallows; goblin clans that dug in generations back are still down there, and her footfalls are turning their old works back up into daylight, thugs and all. The heat and the thirst are the obvious danger; the quieter one is that this is the first place the crew finds proof the blight has a *shape*, not just a smell.

**Mireholt Fens** — Wet, low ground where the blight doesn't dry out between visits — it pools here the way water does, which is why the fen is the thickest trouble on the whole route for its size. Hags and swamp-things aren't passing through, they've made a wet, rotting home of the exact low ground the Longmother has to cross, and they are not happy to have her overhead. This is where the crawl can admit, out loud, that the vanguard's job is getting harder faster than the route is getting easier.

**Frostmaw Caverns** — The road climbs, and for once the danger isn't what her feet stir up — it's what's already waiting under the frost, down where the mineral veins run that feed the caravan's own mana casks. Cold keeps things slow, including the blight, which has let whatever's down here grow old undisturbed instead of being swept along with everything else. This is the turn toward the finale: colder, deeper, quieter, and the first time the crew finds something that was clearly *built*, not just buried.

**Veshker Ruins** — A dead city, stone instead of carapace, built by people who tried to do what the town does without ever learning to walk. It failed the way they all fail — except here, something in it never let go of the refusal to move on. The Vampire Lord is what's left of a ruler who chose to stay and found a way to keep "staying" going long after staying should have killed him. He isn't interested in outrunning the blight; he wants the Longmother to stop, so he can finally be still again on something that won't rot under him. This is the climax: not outrunning a monster, but refusing to make the same mistake the ruins already made.

## 4. Connective tissue to existing systems

- **The Demon Lord** (existing drop-table source, generic enemy archetype) isn't a throned villain waiting in one room — he's what the blight looks like when it finds a strong enough host to wear, which is why "Demon Lord"-tagged fighters turn up plainly, repeatedly, on more than one leg of the route instead of guarding a single lair. The Vampire Lord is the blight's most patient success, not its only one.
- **Camp / Caravan**: the wagons already are the Longmother's shed plates, re-decked; "Caravan" upgrades (Armory Wagon, Mana Casks, Strongbox) read naturally as *what the crews have built out of what she's given them,* which costs gold because salvage and smithing aren't free, not because of abstract game-economy.
- **Armor plates, pincers, weapon shatter**: covered above — gear from her molts, pincers copied from her brood, shatter because chitin doesn't behave like forged steel. None of it needs new mechanics, only a line of flavor text each.

- Is the blight a single intentional enemy (a "the Demon Lord," proper noun, singular) or a condition/plague with no one author, as drafted here? Changes whether there's ever a single final confrontation beyond the Vampire Lord.
- Confirm or veto avoiding "Undearth" — happy to work the struck name back in literally if the owner actually wants it revived rather than reinterpreted.
- Naming: "the Longmother" is a placeholder; needs an owner-approved name for the spider and (if wanted) the town itself.
